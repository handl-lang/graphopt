# Referee — do the two worlds speak the same language?

**Method.** Op vocabularies read from the printed form of every shipped rule: `mlopt_core.laws.all_rules()` on the tensor side, `KERNEL_RULES` + `KERNEL_PRIOR_ART_RULES` on the kernel side. A law whose ops are absent from the other bank cannot be derived there at all; presence is necessary, not sufficient (that needs the proof engine).

## Findings

### NEGATIVE — The two domains share one rule vocabulary, so a law minted in one can be judged by the other.

Jaccard 0.17 over 121 ops — the banks are largely disjoint vocabularies

### NEGATIVE — A kernel law can be re-derived in the tensor bank.

28/49 kernel ops (57%) have no tensor spelling — those laws cannot cross without translation

### WIN — The derivability union the novelty sieve computes is a real floor, not a formality.

3/3 shipped kernel laws are derivable from the 374-rule union — the floor demotes them to known

### WIN — Crossing works through explicit denotation rows: a kernel law is provable in the union even though the tensor bank cannot spell its fused op.

every derivable law has a prior-art witness (`pa_kf_*_expand`) — translation rows, not a shared vocabulary, are what make the union real

## What this establishes, and what it does not

- It establishes that the two banks are **largely disjoint vocabularies**. A shared referee is possible only over the shared slice unless translation rules are added.
- The vocabulary proxy is sufficient for *impossibility*, not necessary for *possibility*: a law can be provable through a denotation row even when its fused op is absent from the other bank. `referee_proof.md` is that run.
- It says nothing about whether a *player* transfers; that is the schema question.
